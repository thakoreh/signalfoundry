# Clientrove product direction: first assessment

> Product direction update: the clarified goal is a prospect-to-conversation-to-meeting workflow, with discovery and drafts as its first stage. This earlier review describes the current interface and initial validation work. See the newer outreach journey report for the expanded direction and US$25 total monthly testing constraint.

October 19, 2026. This is a market-position hypothesis for discussion, not validated demand, a launch promise, or a profitability forecast. No customers were contacted and no paid research calls or purchases were made.

## The opportunity to test

**Make a B2B SaaS founder's weekly prospecting work materially easier.** The user has accepted B2B SaaS founders as the initial hypothesis, conditional on useful results and willingness to pay. There is no existing personal tester cohort, so recruiting qualified users is part of the experiment. Start with founders who have a launched product, can name a company buyer, and already try to reach businesses. Asking a general AI tool to “find customers” is easy to try; getting a consistently relevant, reachable, fresh shortlist that the founder acts on is the harder product.

“Agent-first” describes delivery, not a defensible customer benefit. The offer to test is: **“A fresh shortlist of businesses that match your offer, with evidence and a practical way to contact them.”** Essentialness would come from repeatable time savings and better targeting inside an existing sales habit. A pleasant first search, a CSV download, or market growth does not establish that.

## Initial buyer hypothesis

| Segment | Why it might work | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B2B SaaS founders with a live offer and explicit business buyer | Matches the user's preferred audience and website-first workflow; a concise research loop could save founder time. | Whether target companies can be reliably identified and reached, and whether founders actually prospect repeatedly. |
| Owner-led specialist B2B service agencies/consultancies | Alternative cohort with a clear offer and existing prospecting work. | Not the selected initial audience; there is no evidence they are more accessible to the user or more willing to pay. |
| Local B2B service providers | Public business sites/contact routes may be sufficient and geography can be specific. | Reachability may depend on phone/in-person work rather than email; the business may not prospect regularly. |
| Sales operations teams and lead-generation agencies | Frequent use and clear operational needs. | Mature tooling, higher scale/integration requirements, and stronger expectations than the present product meets. |

**Provisional initial focus: founder-led B2B SaaS with a usable product, a defined company buyer, and an existing outbound experiment.** Narrow further to one buyer type once the first cohort reveals coverage and pain. A pre-idea founder needing product validation, a consumer app seeking individual installs, and an established sales team needing enterprise CRM operations are different jobs. They should not be combined into the same first test. These boundaries are product hypotheses, not evidence of willingness to pay. Kitchen Sense and Explee are test/reference examples, not the user's business or proof of our target market.

The first promise should be concrete: **“Turn your SaaS website into a sourced shortlist of businesses to approach this week.”** The business buyer and region must remain editable. A website can explain the seller; it does not prove who needs the product. Do not advertise autonomous sales, guaranteed customers, verified emails when verification is unavailable, or hundreds of results while the testing cap remains five.

## The asset worth building

The asset is an accumulating understanding of which businesses the user can use: accepted/rejected candidates and reasons, previously approached companies, current customers, evidence freshness, and contact outcomes. With permission and enough real feedback, this can improve the next shortlist and prevent repeats. It is not a proprietary database or a learning system yet; neither should be claimed today.

The first differentiator to earn is **less vetting per useful business**. Test whether the user can understand fit, inspect sources, find a contact route, and decide what to do without reopening every website. Contact-first sorting helps, but an irrelevant company with a public email must still fail relevance checks. A draft should use evidenced facts, not invented buying intent.

Retention requires a genuine reason to return: new eligible companies or meaningfully refreshed evidence, preferences that improve with feedback, and continuity with what the user has already done. Repackaging the same static list every week is not recurring value. If one useful export solves the whole job, a one-off paid research product may fit better than a subscription.

The current app has website onboarding, bounded public-web discovery, conservative rules-based candidate checks, contact status, CSV export, and an explicitly requested LLM message draft. It has no validated customer-quality benchmark, CRM connection, sending workflow, or autonomous research agent. Five results is the current testing cap. This brief proposes a direction; it does not imply those missing capabilities are built.

## First customer experiment without a personal tester network

Paid acquisition can recruit a learning cohort, but cannot resolve product quality by itself. **A signup means acquisition worked; a useful first shortlist means activation; a second useful batch means some recurring value; a payment and renewal test willingness to pay.** Keep those outcomes separate.

First make the test public and honest: a stable public landing page, secure real accounts or a clearly labeled research-pilot signup, appropriate data handling, working attribution, and a tested core action. A local demo URL and local authentication handoff cannot serve real ad traffic. If production signup and the engine are not ready, collect interest for a disclosed pilot; do not disguise a waitlist as a functioning paid app.

Proposed acquisition test, not a campaign launch:

1. Test one message for founder-led **B2B SaaS** in one supported language/market. Google Search is a plausible first channel for explicit problem queries; the hypothesis is that intent is easier to inspect than with a broad audience campaign. It does not guarantee the searcher is a SaaS founder. Review actual search terms and ask a short buyer-stage qualification question. One alternative is a permitted placement in a relevant founder newsletter/community, tested separately rather than mixing channels.
2. Agree one risk budget and a review date before any account work. **US$150 in total is an illustrative learning-budget ceiling only**, not a CPC estimate, a user recommendation to spend now, or a promise it will recruit five qualified testers. A short ad window must still allow roughly two weeks to observe subsequent product use. If the budget yields too little qualified traffic, report the test as inconclusive rather than treating silence as proof of no demand.
3. Start with five **qualified activated founders**, not five arbitrary signups: a real B2B offer, a defined buyer, and one completed research run they can assess. Ask for a recent prospecting example and its current time cost. Each reviews a five-candidate batch for fit, source correctness, contact usability, and vetting minutes. Do not infer outreach permission or successful delivery from discovering an address.
4. Offer those qualified users a small disclosed paid pilot for two weekly shortlists, initially five candidates each, with clear shortfall/refund rules and an editable, fact-grounded message draft for accepted prospects. **US$49 total is a test-offer hypothesis**, not a launch price or known willingness to pay. Five results is a quality preview/testing unit, not evidence that this volume is valuable enough to buy. If users regard the deliverable as too small, record that objection rather than silently promising hundreds. Use confirmed data rights and expressly authorized provider usage. Label concierge assistance and count its labor cost. A working payment flow, honest offer, rejection handling, and stated refund terms would be prerequisites; this research has not implemented checkout or a refund policy.
5. Observe return use and ask for a paid continuation with a clear offer. Do not add autonomous sending or a CRM to rescue a weak shortlist result. No ads, placements, outreach, accounts, budgets, or purchases have been created by this research.

Google documents that primary conversion actions can drive bidding, while secondary actions are generally observational; custom goals are an exception. Configure a future test around the deepest clean event with sufficient data, not an unrelated account-default goal. Do not assume a rare purchase event is ready for automated optimization in a tiny cohort. [Google conversion-action guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11461796).

For Search, inspect the terms that actually triggered ads and exclude irrelevant intent. [Google search-terms guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13738234). Budget implementation must match the authorized ceiling: for most campaigns, an average daily budget can incur up to twice that amount in a day and 30.4 times it in a month. It is not a strict total-test cap; confirm available total-budget controls or an appropriate monitored stop mechanism before launch. [Google budget guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486536).

## Measure the product funnel before scaling acquisition

| Stage | What counts | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent and qualified signup | Attributable visitor describes a live B2B SaaS offer and company buyer | Cheap traffic or email capture does not establish a useful product. |
| Website onboarding | Real business URL analyzed and editable target confirmed | A generated profile is not accepted prospect quality. |
| First value | Founder accepts at least one relevant business with a usable contact route and accurate supporting evidence | A completed API request or five displayed rows is not activation. |
| Action | Export selected accepted businesses or request/use a factual draft | A download or draft is not outreach, a reply, or revenue. |
| Return | Another meaningful research/action session in the next 7–14 days | Logging in alone does not establish recurring value. |
| Paid intent and revenue | Explicit price acceptance; then actual payment, refund status, and renewal tracked separately | Survey enthusiasm is weaker than a purchase, and a purchase is weaker than retained use. |

Measure unique workspaces/cohorts, campaign/source, dates, buyer stage, and geography. Deduplicate events and validate a real test flow before importing anything as an Ads conversion. Track signup-to-activation and activation-to-return independently. Do not send prospect contact data or research snippets into advertising analytics. Paid acquisition profitability remains unknown until the relevant downstream events, delivery cost, and paid continuation exist.

Suggested pre-agreed decision gates for this tiny pilot—not industry benchmarks or statistical proof:

| Gate | Initial pass signal |
| --- | --- |
| Relevance and evidence | At least 4 of 5 candidates accepted as relevant by each user in most batches; no fabricated company/contact facts. Track serious factual errors separately. |
| Reachability | At least 3 of 5 have a contact route that this customer considers usable. Show published/verified status distinctly; verify email if that is part of the paid promise. |
| Time saved | Median vetting time per accepted candidate at least 50% lower than the customer's observed baseline. |
| Paid demand | At least 3 of 5 pay for the pilot; record who declines and why. Friends' compliments or free usage do not count. |
| Recurring value | At least 3 of 5 use the second batch and at least 2 purchase a stated continuation. Separate engagement from commercial renewal. |
| Sustainable delivery | Fully loaded delivery cost leaves room at the tested price, including provider charges, failed attempts, infrastructure, payment costs, and human review. |

Track **cost per qualified reachable lead** as total attempted-work cost divided by unique customer-accepted relevant businesses with a usable contact route. Also track a stricter verified-email metric when relevant. Keep this delivery metric distinct from acquisition: **paid-media cost per activated founder = ad spend / activated founders; paid-media CAC = ad spend / acquired paying customers**. Fully loaded CAC also includes acquisition labor, creative, tools, and other acquisition-channel costs. With zero payers, report spend and zero purchases instead of inventing a finite CAC. Payback requires contribution after variable delivery, payment/refund, and material support costs, together with observed retention; a hypothetical lifetime value is not evidence.

If quality is good but repeat use is weak, test one-off research. If reachability fails, improve the specific data gap before increasing volume. If a useful result still does not attract payment, reconsider the buyer/problem instead of assuming more automation will fix demand. Cheap clicks from unsupported markets or unrelated search intent should not determine the product direction.

Five users provide directional evidence only. Expand to a broader cohort and repeat across several cycles before public outcome claims, fixed allowances, acquisition spend, or a profitability conclusion. More detailed cost and supplier-rights notes are in [AGENT_PRODUCT_AND_PRICING.md](./AGENT_PRODUCT_AND_PRICING.md).

## Recommended next step

Prepare **one B2B founder pilot offer and a trustworthy end-to-end measurement path**, then recruit a bounded cohort after readiness checks. The offer is an outcome to evaluate: businesses matching the founder's stated buyer, an evidenced usable contact route, a factual first-message draft, and another useful batch the following week. “Qualified” means accepted against the user's explicit buyer criteria, not proof of buying intent.

The validation loop is: **qualified founder → real product/target → reviewed shortlist → usable contact and draft/export → return use → actual payment/renewal → measured delivery and acquisition cost**. Honest rejections and refunds are evidence too. Decide whether to improve, narrow, sell one-off research, or stop before expanding features or ad spend. The immediate deliverable is a measurable pilot, not a profitability claim or a plan to build every sales feature.
